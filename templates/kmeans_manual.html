How to Perform Each Iteration

Apply the same three steps to all 100 records.

  1. Measure distance: for a record (x, y) and centroid (a, b), calculate the Euclidean distance: d = √((x − a)² + (y − b)²).
  2. Assign the record: compare its distance to C1, C2, and C3, then assign it to the closest centroid.
  3. Update each centroid: average the social media hours and screen time of the records assigned to that cluster: Ck = (mean(x), mean(y)).
  4. Evaluate the assignment: sum the squared distance from every record to its assigned centroid. This is the within-cluster SSE.

Repeat assignment and update using the new centroids. The centroid and SSE tables below show the supplied results; the record-level distance and assignment results require the missing 100-row input stated above.

Within-Cluster Variance

Distinct from SSE.

A valid within-cluster variance comparison cannot be calculated from the supplied results alone. SSE is the total of squared distances to assigned centroids; variance requires the individual record values and their cluster assignments (or equivalent per-cluster sums and counts) for each iteration. Those iteration-level inputs are not available, so no variance values are reported.

Final Interpretation

Across the three reported updates, SSE decreases from 168.79 to 159.22, indicating a lower total squared distance under the supplied results. C1 is unchanged between Iterations 2 and 3, while C2 and C3 still move, so Iteration 3 is the last reported step rather than evidence that every centroid has converged.
